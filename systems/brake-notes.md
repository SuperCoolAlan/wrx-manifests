## Rotors — RESOLVED 2026-08-19

**Part numbers used: KNS4651 (front) + KNS4656 (rear).** Made by **DBA (Disc Brakes Australia)** for KNS Brakes. NOT the 2011 donor rotors — an earlier recollection of "2011 rotors" was mistaken; the written note was correct.

### KNS4651 — front, 4-pot gravel rotor
Size and shape of a standard WRX rotor, but with a **larger center bore** and **dual-drilled 5x100 / 5x114.3**, so it bolts to any STI (or 15-21 WRX) front hub. This is the bridge part that makes 06-07 WRX 4-pot calipers work on 5x114.3 STI hubs.

### KNS4656 — rear, 2-pot gravel rotor
**Outer diameter and thickness of a 2006-07 WRX** rear rotor, but with the **parking brake drum sized for the R180 STI rear end**. The 2011 swap brought the R180 and STI rear hubs, so this rotor pairs the 06-07 WRX rear caliper to an STI rear end *and* keeps the e-brake functional. Dual-drilled 5x100 / 5x114.3.

Net: rear caliper and rear rotor OD are both 06-07 WRX spec — an OEM-correct pairing. **Fitment is correct by design. No pad-sweep or clearance check needed.**

### Construction
- **DBA XG-150 high-carbon alloy** — high-performance friction and wear characteristics
- **"Kangaroo Paw" pillar vane venting** — diamond and tear-drop pillars instead of straight vanes; DBA claims up to 20% better heat dissipation than straight-vane. Fronts vented similar to the Subaru Group N rotor.
- Genuinely good discs; resist the heat-checking and judder that kills cheap rotors on track.

### The catch for THIS build
These are **gravel rally rotors** — the diameter is deliberately held down so 15" gravel wheels clear the brakes (popular 15" gravel wheels cap around 300mm of rotor). Alan is on 17"+ tarmac and does not have that constraint. **Good metallurgy on a deliberately-undersized disc.** Gravel duty cycle (lower speeds, short stops, dirt cooling) is also the opposite load case from a 500whp tarmac track car.

Sold individually at most vendors — budget per corner when replacing.

**Vendors:** FastWRX, Flatirons Tuning, Bleeding Tarmac. Manufacturer: knsbrakes.com

## Assessment for This Build

**Verdict: sufficient for now, plan the upgrade.**

What the 4/2-pot swap actually bought vs the stock 04 setup:

- Fixed 4-piston front caliper instead of a sliding single-pot — much stiffer, even pad pressure across the pad face, no slide-pin binding. Real gain in pedal feel and consistency.
- Larger pad footprint — more friction area, slower wear.
- **Rear is the underrated half:** 266mm solid → ~290mm vented is a genuine upgrade.

What it did **not** buy, and why it matters here:

- **Front rotor diameter is essentially unchanged from the stock 04 WRX (~294mm).** Front thermal mass is the number that governs a 500whp track car, and it did not move. No pad compound fixes a rotor too small to absorb the energy. Expect consistent early laps and progressive fade as heat soaks over a session.

Caliper count is not the limiter — brake torque is already enough to lock the tires at any speed. **Heat capacity and pad compound are the limiters.**

## Phase 1 — This Season (interim, on current hardware)

| Item | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stainless lines | ✅ **DONE** | |
| Front brake ducts | ☐ TO DO | **Highest-value remaining item.** See below. |
| Track pads | ☐ TO DO | Carbotech XP8/XP10, Hawk DTC-60, or PFC 08 — all listed for 06-07 WRX |
| High-temp fluid | ☐ TO DO | Motul RBF600/660 or Castrol SRF. **Flush before every event, not every season.** |
| Caliper piston + boot check | ☐ TO DO | Once, before first event. 2007 US car, so low-risk, but a sticking piston you can't feel on the street will cook a corner on track. |

**Pad availability is a non-issue.** 06-07 WRX is a common USDM application — Carbotech, Hawk, PFC, StopTech all support it. (Earlier concern about thin track-compound selection applied to JDM Version 5/6 calipers and does not apply here.)

**Front brake ducts — why this is the priority.** It's fabrication rather than a part you order, which is exactly why it gets skipped. With a 294mm front rotor you are leaning on airflow to compensate for missing thermal mass. GD front bumper has usable lower openings; standard approach is 3" ducting to a backing plate at the hub. This is the difference between fading on lap 4 and running consistent all session.

Keep a separate street pad set — endurance compounds are miserable cold.

## Phase 2 — Winter Project: 04-07 STI Brembos

**Direct bolt-on.** Front knuckles are 05-07 STi, so the **GD-era (04-07) STI Brembo** is the caliper that brackets up — **NOT the 08-14 GR caliper.** Hubs are already 5x114.3, so no conversion.

### Parts
- Front: **04-07 STI Brembo 4-piston calipers** + **326mm x 30mm rotors, 5x114.3**
- Rear: 04-07 STI Brembo 2-piston calipers + ~316mm rotors, 5x114.3
- Brake lines: GD/STI length — do not reuse the current set
- Pads: full track-compound selection available for the Brembo shape

### Caliper year nuance
The 04-07 STI Brembo **caliper and bracket are common across those years** — only the hub bolt pattern changed (04 = 5x100, 05-07 = 5x114.3). **Bolt pattern lives in the rotor/hub, not the caliper**, so a caliper off any 04-07 STI works. Just make sure the **rotors** are the 5x114.3 326mm parts.

Supply is thinner than the 08-14 sets but still common. Buy on caliper condition; rebuild seals if history is unknown.

### Verify before/during

- **Wheel clearance for 326mm.** 17" is the floor, but barrel shape matters as much as diameter. Test-fit before committing.
- **Master cylinder.** Going to 326/316 increases fluid displacement again. If the pedal goes long after a proper bleed, that's displacement, not air — the STI master cylinder is the fix. **Bore sizes need verification against a parts catalog; not recorded here because they haven't been confirmed.**
